# Notes from _Symbols, Selves, and Social Reality_
A Symbolic Interactionist Approach to Social Psychology and Sociology 
 
## Chapter 3: People as Symbol Makers and Users - Language, Naming, and the Construction of Reality

---
- Language is the key medium through which people share meanings and construct "reality".
- **Language is a system of symbols that members of a culture use for representation and communication**
- Language is the source of the symbols we use to give meanings to objects, events, or people and to convey these meanings to ourselves and others  
- Language contains arbitrary and abstract _symbols_ / _features_

---
- _Words_ have meaning on their own but also have meaning when joined with other words. And words serve as the basis for other symbols
- Words facilitate our ability to communicate and share meanings (exercise: Try and tell a friend something about yourself without using spoken or written words. Tell them your plans for the weekend or how your day was. Even simpler - try telling someone what day it is without using spoken or written words)
- Words are important because they offer shared names or categories through which we give meanings to our experiences and share these meanings
- Even though words _facilitate_ our ability to communicate and act,  they do not necessarily make it _easy_ for us to interact with others
- A number of words we use often, such as "a while", "later", and "sometime" have imprecise meanings and can lead to misinterpretations that result in frustrating or ineffective interaction

---
- In addition to containing abstract and arbitrary symbols knows as "words," human languages have another essential feature: grammar or _syntax_
- ==Syntax== is a set of rules for combining symbols to produce more extended meaning
- Using _syntax_ allows us to indicate who is talking or acting, to indicate when something happened, to ask a question, to disagree, to speak about things that haven't happened yet or could never happen, and to construct alternative visions of what might happen if we do this or, on the other hand, do that.
- Syntax enables us to use symbols to _think_, and to communicate thoughts to others

## Chapter 6: Role Taking, Role Making, and the Coordination - Roles, Role Taking, and Role Making

---
- ==Roles== tell us what obligations and expectations apply to our behavior when we occupy a particular social status in a particular setting
- example: knowing if the person standing next to you at the grocery store is an employee or fellow customer)
- Roles carry a _normative force_.... We feel obligated to do what our roles demand of us and are ashamed if we fall short

- ==ROLE TAKING== is an imaginative process where we look at ourselves and our actions from _another persons standpoint_ 
- In order for us to coordinate our actions with the actions of those who are linked with us in a ==reciprocal network of roles== we _MUST_ engage in role taking
- We rely on role taking as we define the situation we find ourselves in, _assuming the perspectives of others_ to detect the role they are projecting, to coordinate our proposed lines of action with theirs, and to negotiate the meaning of our interactions with them.
- Role taking efforts always involve some guesswork and are never completely accurate
