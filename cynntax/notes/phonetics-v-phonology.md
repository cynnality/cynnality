## What separates _phonetics_ from _phonology_?

#### Using **WIND INSTRUMENTS** as an analogy
I could be entirely wrong in the way(s) I'm equating things / drawing comparisons. Just trying to put words to my guess for now to help myself _directionally_ as far as moving towards a more accurate understanding.

**Phonetics**: Concerns the physical production, acoustic transmission and perception of the sounds/signs of language

**Phonology**: Describes the way _outcomes_ of phonetic productions function within a given language or across languages to encode meaning

**Phonetics** observes the (individual) instrument, ==_mechanism_==, physical object (generally)
==concerned with _how_ the instrument is _played_, the _qualities_/_attributes(?)_ of the sound it produces==

**Phonology** observes _outcomes_ that the ==_mechanism_== produces
==concerned with how _fundamentals are applied_ to instruments==

Every instrument _plays "notes"_ and has it's own specific "mechanism". The builds vary across instruments in ways that directly attribute to the sounds they create individually. And each instrument also requires a specific embouchure (how to form the mouth to play the instrument)
So "playing one note" /  individual "notes" would belong to phonetics. 
But, _trills_ would belong to phonology.
It also seems like phonology has a bit more flexibility in terms of it's "scope". It could look at trills for flutes specifically, observing how it's used as a _phrase_ within a larger _section_ as a way to contribute to the overall _feeling_ the section is meant to evoke. It could _also_ look at how trills are applied across different instruments as _phrases_ contributing the the larger _sections_ _feeling_.
