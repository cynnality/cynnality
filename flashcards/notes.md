## economic differences between north and south
- id: 2economic-differences-cw
- Q: How did the North and South differ economically before the Civil War?
  A: The North was industrializing and using railroads for transportation while the South was still rural and using rivers for transport. The South was also dependent on slave labor while the North had started becoming dependent on wage labor (workers getting paid money for their work/labor). The North was profiting from exporting manufactured goods and the South was profiting from an agricultural good - cotton.

## compromise of 1850
- id: 10-11compromise-1850
- Q: What were the main points in the Compromise of 1850?
  A: Territory gained by the U.S. in its conflict with Mexico would be given popular soveignty to decide on allowing slavery or not to please the north and stricter terms were put on the Fugitive Slave Act of 1793 to please the South

- Q: Fugitive Slave Acts
  A: Laws regarding enslaved people that had runaway to the North to escape slavery. The first Fugitive Slave Act was created and passed in 1793. In the Compromise of 1850 the terms of the Fugitive Slave Act were revised and made the law stricter on runaways and anyone helping them escape.

 - Q: The Compromise of 1850 tried to please both the north and the south. Why was the South okay with getting stricter terms on the Fugitive Slave Act opposed to a guarantee of the admittance of a new slave-state? 
  A: The south was satisfied with the compromise and the stricter Fugitive Slave Act terms because they had felt for a long time that the North was blatantly ignoring the Fugitive Slave Act by aiding escaped slaves in their journey to freedom. The stricter terms made it a federal crime to help a fugitive slave escape but it also gave incentives for people to turn fugitives into the authorities so the South felt the resistance to the law in the North would stop.

- id: 18dred-scott-cw

- Q: In 1857, what act of Congress was declared unconstitutional by the United States Supreme Court in the Dred Scott decision?
  A: The Missouri Compromise of 1820. The Court ruled that Congress had no power to prohibit slavery in the territories.
  Mode: questions, dig deep
  Level: 2
  Subject: concept, law/legislation/policy, event

  Tags: compromise, slavery, supreme court case, sectional crisis, civil war specific cause, politics

- Q: What is the significance of the Supreme Court's ruling in the Dred Scott case?
  A: The ruling declared that living in a free-state did not make a slave free, slaves were considered property under the Constitution, all African Americans, even if they were free, were not considered U.S. citizens, and most importantly; it ruled that the Missouri Compromise of 1820 was unconstitutional since slaves were considered property. By overturning the Missouri Compromise, the Supreme Court had cleared the way for the extension of slavery into new territories which the South happily cheered on.

- Q: Compare and contrast the Nullification Crisis of 1832 with South Carolina's secession in 1860.
  A: Both were attempts by South Carolina to assert states' rights over federal authority. However, the Nullification Crisis was about a tariff and was resolved with a compromise, while the secession crisis was about slavery and led directly to the Civil War, with Lincoln refusing to compromise on the idea of an unbreakable Union.
