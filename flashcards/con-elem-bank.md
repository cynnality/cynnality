## necessary-and-proper-clause 
name: Necessary and Proper Clause
article: article-1
definition: Grants Congress the power to do things that aren't specifcally stated in the constitution. in section 8 of article 1, there is a list of clearly defined powers of Congress and the last item on this list says that congress can do anything 'necessary and proper' to performing any of the powers listed. It's very vague and could be interpreted to mean a lot of different things. Since it is the Supreme Court's main job to interpret the constitution, when they decided in McCulloch v. Maryland that this "necessary and proper" clause meant Congress had implied powers (powers that are not specifically listed in the Constitution)
cases:
  - mcculloch-maryland
  - gibbons-ogden

## commerce-clause
name: Commerce Clause
article: article-1
definition: This clause mainly gives the federal government the power to control and regulate trade to and from the states. So while states do have the power to control and regulate trade within it's own borders, any trade that travels over state lines is regulated and controlled by the federal government
cases:
  - gibbons-ogden

## three-fifths-compromise
name: Three-Fifths Compromise
article: article-1
definition: A set of things in article one that were added to help southern pro-slavery people accept the constitution before its official acceptance. Essentially they say that the government can't ban slave-trade until 25 years after the acceptance of the constitution, amongst other terms relating to giving pro-slavery people some powers.
cases:
  - plessy-ferguson

## judicial-review
name: Judicial Review
article: article-3
definition: This idea was established in the supreme courts Marbury v. Madison decision in 1803. It's one of the most foundational elements/ideas of what the supreme court does so it is a very complex topic. At its most basic it means that the Supreme Court has the power to review federal law (laws passed by congress/legislative branch), and when it does review federal law, if the court finds that the law conflicts with the constitution, the supreme court has the power to strike the law down. 
cases:
  - marbury-madison

## supremacy-clause
name: Supremacy Clause
article: article-6
definition: This clause says that when federal and state laws conflict, the federal law is stronger than the state law. This does not mean that state courts must also follow this clause. this only applies to the federal level courts when it comes to 2 laws that conflict with each other and 1 is federal law and the other is state law.
cases:
  - gibbons-ogden
  - mcculloch-maryland

## symbolic-speech

amendment: amendment-1
