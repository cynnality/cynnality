# Print Project Workflow

Although every project was different, most print projects followed a similar process from initial order to finished product.

## Order Review

Projects would either come directly from customers I was working with or be passed along from the customer service desk.

The first step was reviewing the order details and gathering any artwork, logos, specifications, or previous project files that might be relevant. In many cases I would contact the customer directly to clarify design requirements, discuss product specifications, or request additional files.

## Design & Proofing

Every project received a proof before production.

Sometimes the proof contained a single design option if the project specifications were already well defined. Other times I prepared multiple concepts to help customers compare different approaches and determine what best matched their goals.

Proofs were revised as needed until final approval was received and documented.

## Production Preparation

Once a proof was approved, I prepared the artwork for production.

This included setting up cut lines, bleed areas, registration marks, and any other production requirements. For projects involving multiple products, such as sticker runs or sign orders, the artwork was arranged to maximize material efficiency while still allowing proper spacing for cutting and finishing.

The completed production file was then prepared for printing and sent to RIP software where color profiles, print settings, material settings, and production queues were configured.

## Printing

Projects were printed using materials selected specifically for the product being produced.

Different materials, print settings, and workflows were required depending on whether the project involved stickers, signs, banners, decals, or other products.

## Drying, Laminating & Finishing

After printing, projects entered the finishing stage.

Drying times varied depending on the type of print and material being used. Full-color vinyl prints typically required extended drying time and often received a laminated protective layer before cutting and finishing.

Many projects required additional production steps such as contour cutting, trimming, weeding, transfer tape application, mounting, assembly, or other finishing processes depending on the final product.

## Final Review

Before delivery, finished products were reviewed to ensure they matched the approved proof and production specifications.

Only after this final review would projects be packaged, organized, and prepared for customer pickup or installation.
